## Research Projects

### 1. Time-Series Remaining Useful Life (RUL) Benchmark

**Repository:** [time-series-rul-benchmark](https://github.com/sadra-github/time-series-rul-benchmark)

I am developing a reproducible benchmark for Remaining Useful Life prediction using multivariate time-series data from the NASA C-MAPSS FD001 dataset.

The project investigates the role of temporal representation in RUL prediction and emphasizes transparent experimental protocols and reliable model evaluation.

**Key objectives:**
- Establish reproducible machine learning baselines.
- Evaluate the influence of temporal window representations.
- Apply appropriate data splitting and preprocessing procedures to prevent data leakage.
- Compare predictive models using established regression metrics.
- Maintain transparent configurations and reproducible experiments.

**Research areas:** Time-Series Analysis, Predictive Maintenance, Machine Learning, and Prognostics.

## Research Philosophy and Working Principles

My approach to research is based on scientific rigor, reproducibility, methodological transparency, and evidence-based evaluation.

I consider reliable model evaluation as important as predictive performance. My research approach emphasizes appropriate data splitting, leakage prevention, transparent preprocessing, systematic baseline comparison, and suitable evaluation metrics.

I am interested in understanding not only whether a model performs well, but also why it performs well, under which conditions its performance remains reliable, and how its limitations can be identified. I value careful interpretation of experimental results and avoid making claims that extend beyond the available evidence.

Where appropriate, I explore the integration of mathematical modeling and physical knowledge with data-driven learning. My goal is to develop machine learning methodologies that are scientifically defensible and relevant to real-world engineering systems.

I also value open science and responsible research communication. I aim to share public code, documented experiments, and reproducible benchmarks while protecting private datasets, unpublished findings, and confidential research materials.
